Significance Statement
======================

Target length: ~120 words (plain language, for the QST submission form).
Audience: a scientifically literate non-specialist reader, e.g. a journal
editor outside the QML sub-field, or a science journalist.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Quantum machine learning at near-term hardware scale routinely fails
to train on hard prediction tasks, and the failure is typically
attributed to "barren plateaus" -- a phenomenon in which the gradients
needed to train the quantum model vanish exponentially. We show that
on a benchmark chaotic-prediction task at the relevant hardware scale,
this explanation is wrong. Direct gradient measurements rule out the
barren-plateau regime by four orders of magnitude. The failure is
instead caused by the quantum model simply being too small to express
the trajectory it is asked to fit. We then show that an alternative
quantum architecture -- a fixed quantum reservoir paired with a
classical linear readout -- bypasses both failure modes by
construction, achieving 81% lower error and training fifty thousand
times faster on the same benchmark.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Word count: 122.

Notes for the submitter:
- This statement deliberately leads with the diagnosis, not the
  comparison numbers, because the diagnosis is the QST-worthy
  contribution. If a reviewer sees only this paragraph, they should
  walk away with "this paper changes how the field thinks about
  variational quantum failure," not "this paper is yet another
  benchmark."
- The "fifty thousand times faster" is a true number but stated
  carefully: it is the algorithmic ratio of closed-form ridge to
  iterative variational training, not a hardware-driven speedup.
- The "four orders of magnitude" framing is the cleanest one-line
  defence against the BP-rebuttal. It is the same number that appears
  in the abstract.
